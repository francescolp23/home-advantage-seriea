**Behind Closed Doors: Home Advantage and Referee Bias in Serie A**

This repository contains an econometric study investigating the impact of crowd presence on professional football outcomes. Leveraging match-level data from Serie A (2018-2023), the project examines the "natural experiment" provided by the Covid-19 pandemic to isolate the effect of fans on match performance and officiating.

Key Highlights:

Methodology: Employs a Difference-in-Differences (DiD) approach with Team and Season Fixed Effects to control for unobserved heterogeneity.

Data: 3,800 team-match observations covering five seasons.

Core Finding: The analysis reveals a fascinating dichotomy. While home advantage in goals and win probability remained resilient even in empty stadiums, the disciplinary advantage (yellow card difference) almost entirely vanished.

Conclusion: The results suggest that crowd pressure primarily influences refereeing decisions (social pressure channel) rather than the structural drivers of home performance (familiarity, travel, or routine).

Tech Stack: Python, Pandas, Linearmodels (Fixed Effects), Matplotlib/Seaborn.
